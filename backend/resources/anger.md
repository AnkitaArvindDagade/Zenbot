# Working with anger and frustration

## Anger as a messenger
Anger often shows up to protect something — a boundary that got crossed, a value that got ignored, an unmet need. Asking "what is this anger trying to protect?" can turn a reactive moment into useful information.

## Creating a pause before reacting
Anger produces a real physical surge that peaks and then fades, often within 90 seconds to a few minutes if you don't feed it further. Stepping away, even just to another room, and letting that first wave pass before responding usually leads to a very different outcome than reacting in the heat of the moment.

## The body remembers anger too
Clenched jaw, tight shoulders, a hot face — anger lives in the body as much as the mind. Unclenching your hands, dropping your shoulders, and taking a slower breath can start to physically dial down the intensity even before the thoughts catch up.

## Underneath the anger
It's common for anger to be sitting on top of a softer, more vulnerable feeling — hurt, fear, embarrassment, disappointment. Gently asking yourself what's underneath the anger can open up a more honest conversation, with others and with yourself.
