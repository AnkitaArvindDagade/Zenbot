# Coping with anxiety

## Naming the feeling
When anxiety shows up, it often feels like a vague wave of dread before you can even name it. Simply saying to yourself "this is anxiety" can create a small bit of distance between you and the feeling, which makes it easier to respond instead of react.

## The 4-7-8 breath
A slow breath pattern can calm the nervous system faster than almost anything else. Breathe in through your nose for 4 counts, hold gently for 7 counts, and exhale slowly through your mouth for 8 counts. Repeat it four or five times. The long exhale is what signals your body it's safe to relax.

## Grounding through the senses
When anxious thoughts are spiraling, try naming 5 things you can see, 4 things you can touch, 3 things you can hear, 2 things you can smell, and 1 thing you can taste. This pulls attention out of the "what if" spiral and back into the present moment.

## Worry time
If anxious thoughts keep interrupting your day, try setting aside a specific 10-minute "worry time" later on. When a worry pops up, jot it down and tell yourself you'll think about it properly during that window. This isn't about suppressing worry, it's about giving it a container instead of letting it take over everything.

## Small, steady action
Anxiety often grows in the space of "not doing anything." Picking one small, concrete next step — even something as small as drinking a glass of water or sending one email — can interrupt the freeze response and remind your body that you're capable of moving forward.
