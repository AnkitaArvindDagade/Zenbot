# Loneliness and connection

## Loneliness is about quality, not headcount
It's possible to feel deeply lonely in a room full of people, and perfectly content with very little social contact. Loneliness tends to track how *understood* and *connected* you feel, not how many people are around you.

## Low-stakes reconnection
Reaching back out to someone after a long gap can feel intimidating, like there's a debt to repay. A short, low-pressure message — "thinking of you, how have you been?" — is usually enough to reopen a connection without needing a big explanation.

## Shared activity over deep conversation
Not every connection needs to start with a heart-to-heart. Doing something alongside another person — a walk, a class, a shared hobby — often builds closeness more naturally than trying to force a big emotional conversation right away.

## Being alone vs. being lonely
Solitude and loneliness aren't the same thing. Time alone can be restorative when it's chosen and used well; loneliness is the ache of wanting connection and not having it. Naming which one you're actually feeling can point you toward what will actually help.
