# When to consider reaching out for professional support

## Duration and intensity matter
A rough week is different from a rough few months. If low mood, anxiety, or stress has stuck around most days for several weeks and is affecting sleep, work, or relationships, that's a reasonable point to loop in a doctor or therapist rather than continuing to manage it alone.

## You don't need a crisis to start therapy
Therapy isn't only for when things fall apart — many people start simply because they want a structured space to understand a pattern, process something difficult, or build better coping tools before things get harder.

## Different kinds of support exist
A general doctor can help rule out physical causes and refer you onward. A therapist or counselor offers regular talk-based support. A psychiatrist can assess whether medication might help. There's no single "right" entry point — starting anywhere is a reasonable first step.

## It's okay if the first fit isn't right
Not every therapist or approach will click on the first try, and that's normal, not a sign that support doesn't work for you. It's reasonable to try someone else if the fit doesn't feel right after a few sessions.
