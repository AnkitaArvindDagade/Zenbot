# Sleep and rest

## A wind-down routine signals safety
Bodies sleep best when they get a clear signal that the day is over. Dimming lights, putting screens away 30–60 minutes before bed, and doing something low-key like reading or stretching helps shift the nervous system from "on" to "off."

## If your mind won't quiet down
Lying in bed trying to force sleep usually backfires. If your mind is racing, it can help to get up, sit somewhere dim and quiet, jot down whatever's circling in your head, and go back to bed once you feel a bit more settled rather than staring at the ceiling.

## Caffeine and its long tail
Caffeine can stay active in the body for 6-plus hours, so an afternoon coffee may still be affecting sleep quality that night even if it doesn't feel like it's keeping you awake.

## Consistency matters more than perfection
Going to bed and waking up around the same time each day — even on weekends — trains your body's internal clock more effectively than any single "perfect night" of sleep.

## When sleep struggles feel bigger than tiredness
Ongoing sleep problems can both cause and be caused by anxiety or low mood, so if poor sleep has been persistent for weeks, it's worth mentioning to a doctor rather than only treating it as a lifestyle issue.
