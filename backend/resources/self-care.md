# Everyday self-care

## Self-care is often unglamorous
Real self-care looks less like a spa day and more like eating something today, drinking water, getting a bit of daylight, and going to bed at a reasonable hour. The small, boring basics tend to matter more than any single big gesture.

## Gratitude as a practice, not a feeling
Gratitude doesn't have to mean feeling grateful in the moment — it can just be the practice of writing down one or two specific things, even small ones, that went okay today. Over time this practice can gently shift what your attention tends to notice.

## Movement as mood support
You don't need an intense workout for movement to help your mood — even a 10-minute walk changes brain chemistry in ways that can measurably lift mood and lower stress hormones.

## Protecting unstructured time
Constantly optimizing every hour of the day can quietly add to stress. Leaving some genuinely unstructured time — with no goal, no productivity attached — gives your mind room to actually rest.

## Self-compassion over self-criticism
Talking to yourself the way you'd talk to a friend going through the same thing — rather than the harshest voice in your head — isn't about letting yourself off the hook. It tends to lead to more motivation and follow-through than self-criticism does, not less.
